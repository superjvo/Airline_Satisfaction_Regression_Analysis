# Airline Satisfaction Regression Analysis
Predicting customer satisfaction from Invistico airline data to determine whether upgrading in-flight entertainment would result in an increase in satisfied customers
# Overview
In this scenario the stakeholders at Invistico Airlines are considering upgrading in-flight entertainment throughout the company and would like to know whether the quality of the entertainment options has an impact on customer satisfaction. By taking survey data of 129880 sample observations, a logistic regression model was generated to predict the satisfaction classification using only the entertainment variable in order to find any correlations. The model returned scores of 80% for accuracy and an 82% F1 score. While the model did show a correlation, there was still a relatively high number of false positives indicating there are additional factors that drive customer satisfaction. 
# Data Understanding 
The dataset in this particular project comprised of fictional airline survey data on 129880 independent customer experiences. The initial data consisted of 22 features in total with the primary outcome feature being satisfaction. Customer satisfaction was represented as a binary categorical variable with only 2 possible values (satisfied or dissatisfied). The project's objective was to investigate whether upgrading entertainment options would lead to higher satisfaction responses. The in-flight entertainment variable was also represented categorically with there being 6 separate options available. Both the outcome variable and the independent variable needed to be transformed into the proper numeric data types since logistic regression models struggle to fit around object/string data types. # Modeling and Evaluation
The logistic regression model incorporated binary outcome variable of satisfaction and a single independent categorical variable being the in-flight entertainment class for that experience. The fitted model returned a coefficient of 0.99, showing that each step up in the category translated to a roughly 171% increase in the probability of a customer being classified as satisfied. The intercept of -3.19 also points to the lowest tier option reducing the probability of satisfaction by 20x which enforces the importance of higher quality entertainment options. The primary metrics used to evaluate the model were accuracy and F1 score. The accuracy score of 80.2% was a decent score as only about 20% of its predictions were incorrect. However, being such a large dataset that number equates to over 25000 incorrect classifications. # Conclusion
The use of logistic regression was able to identify a positive correlation between in-flight entertainment and customer satisfaction when trained on the Invistico Airlines survey data. This alone is not enough to justify a business wide overhaul of entertainment options which would be an extremely costly endeavor. Since the initial model was limited in its scope and contained a considerable amount of false predictions, the business would benefit from a much more thorough investigation into what factors drive customer satisfaction the most. Additional variables such as flight distance, arrival and departure delays, cleanliness, seat comfort, and the variety of support provided would be a great place to start. While some of these factors may not be in the airlines control, the insights uncovered from the ones that can be addressed could result in a more financially sound solution as well as a better overall picture of what contributes to customer satisfaction.
